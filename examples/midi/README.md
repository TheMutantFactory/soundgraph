# MIDI examples

Tunes for the piano roll's **File > Import MIDI**. Provenance, in the spirit of
docs/fm-import-sources.md: every file here must say where it came from and why it
may be here. Everything below is listed by its source as **Public Domain**; pieces
Mutopia offers under Creative Commons licences are deliberately not vendored, so
this folder needs no attribution table.

The roll holds 128 bars of sixteenths — its `MAX_STEPS`, which the reader and the
schema share — and every tune here fits, whole. The importer still says out loud
how many notes a longer piece leaves behind. Bar lines are the roll's, sixteen
steps apart: a piece in 3/4 or 6/8 keeps its timing exactly but its bars do not
land on the roll's lines, which is a known limitation rather than an offset.

- `ode-to-joy.mid` — generated by `tools/make-demo-midi.mjs`. The melody is
  Beethoven's (1824); the encoding is this repository's own. Format 0, 96 ticks
  per quarter — the plainest SMF spelling, which is the point: it is what the
  importer is tested against.
- `bach-invention-08.mid` — J.S. Bach, Invention No. 8 in F major, BWV 779.
  Mutopia Project (mutopiaproject.org/ftp/BachJS/BWV779/bach-invention-08/).
  Format 1, three tracks, 384 ticks per quarter — the real-world case the
  generated demo is not.
- `bach-invention-04.mid` — J.S. Bach, Invention No. 4 in D minor, BWV 775.
  Mutopia (…/BachJS/BWV775/bach-invention-04/). Driving sixteenths, in 3/8.
- `bach-invention-10.mid` — J.S. Bach, Invention No. 10 in G major, BWV 781.
  Mutopia (…/BachJS/BWV781/bach-invention-10/). The fast one — 172 bpm from the
  file.
- `fur-elise.mid` — L. van Beethoven, Für Elise, WoO 59. Mutopia
  (…/BeethovenLv/WoO59/fur_Elise_WoO59/). 905 notes over 40 bars.
- `entertainer.mid` — Scott Joplin, The Entertainer (1902). Mutopia
  (…/JoplinS/entertainer/). The long one: 2621 notes over 76 bars, and the case
  that showed the reader's old sixteen-bar ceiling — it arrived as 560 notes.
- `greensleeves.mid` — Traditional, the hymntune setting for SATB. Mutopia
  (…/Traditional/greensleeves/). Four-voice chords — the polyphony demonstration.
